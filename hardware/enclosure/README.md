# Enclosure (V1: black box)

3D-printable PETG enclosure for the Waveshare **ESP32-S3-LCD-1.3** board plus a small LiPo.
It's generated by a Fusion 360 script, so you change parameters and re-run instead of
editing the model by hand.

Outside size with the default battery: **30.1 × 33.0 × 20.6 mm** (W × H × D).

## Generate it in Fusion 360

1. Fusion → **UTILITIES** → **ADD-INS** → **Scripts and Add-Ins**.
2. Click **+** → *Script or add-in from device* → pick
   `hardware/enclosure/SpotifyDisplayEnclosure/`.
3. Select **SpotifyDisplayEnclosure** → **Run**.

It opens one design tab per mount variant (below). Each tab has three components:
**Shell**, **Lid**, and **Reference (do not print)**, a see-through mock-up of the board,
standoffs, USB-C and battery for checking fit. It also writes `v1_<variant>_shell.stl`
and `v1_<variant>_lid.stl` into `SpotifyDisplayEnclosure/stl/`. A message box reports the
size and any fit warnings. To build fewer variants, trim `VARIANTS` in the script.

## Mount variants

| Variant | What changes | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `top_loop` | Keyring loop centred on the top edge | Keychain that hangs straight |
| `corner_loop` | Keyring loop on the top-left corner | Keychain that hangs as a diamond |
| `belt_clip` | No loop; spring clip on the lid | Slides over a belt, or a belt loop at least 10 mm wide |

- **Keyring loops** have a 3.5 mm hole (split ring or lanyard cord), with the hole 4 mm
  past the edge. They're 5 mm thick and centred in the depth of the closed box, so the
  weight hangs evenly front-to-back. The top loop sits at the left-right centre of mass,
  so the screen hangs level.
- **Belt clip:** a 9 mm wide, 1.8 mm thick spring tongue rooted at the top and open at the
  bottom. It has a 1.2 mm gap and a ramp at the tip, so you push it down over the belt.
  It's narrow so both lid screws stay reachable. If it's too stiff or too loose, change
  `CLIP_T` (stiffness) and `CLIP_GAP` (grip).

To change anything (battery size, wall thickness, USB-C position, heat inserts on/off),
edit the **PARAMETERS** block at the top of `SpotifyDisplayEnclosure.py` and run again.

## Design

- **Front brim:** 1.2 mm thick. The window is the LCD's 23.4 mm active area minus
  0.1 mm per side, offset 1.58 mm toward the top. The brim covers the panel border and the
  black FPC strip at the bottom. The window edge and the front edge are chamfered.
- **Board retention:** the board drops in face-first from the back. Four posts on the lid
  press the brass standoff ends, which holds the glass against the brim.
- **Battery bay:** sits behind the standoffs and between the lid posts. It takes a battery
  up to about 26 × 21.4 mm footprint. The default is a 502025 (5 × 20 × 25 mm, ~200 mAh),
  laid with the 25 mm side across the width.
- **Fasteners:** 2 × M2 × 6 self-tapping screws through a counterbored lid into two
  bosses. The bosses have 45° gussets so they print without supports. Set
  `HEAT_INSERTS = True` for M2 heat-set inserts (3.2 mm OD) and M2 × 6 machine screws.
- **Openings:** USB-C on the bottom edge (with a shallow outside pocket so moulded plugs
  seat fully), a power-switch slot on the top edge, 7 vent slots per side wall level with
  the PCB, and 5 slots in the lid.

## Print (PETG)

- Shell: as modelled, **front face on the bed**, no supports.
- Lid: **flip it** so the flat outside face is on the bed (posts point up), no supports.
- Belt-clip lid: the clip is on the outside, so it can't lie flat. **Stand it on its left
  or right edge** so the layers run along the tongue (laid any other way, the tongue snaps
  at a layer line). Use supports under the four posts and a brim for stability.
- Keyring loops print as part of the shell with no extra supports.
- 0.2 mm layers (0.12–0.16 mm for a nicer front), 4 walls, 20–30 % infill. A smooth PEI
  sheet gives a glossy front, a textured sheet gives a matte one.
- PETG is RF-transparent, so the ceramic antenna is fine. Don't put metal near it
  (this matters for the magnet version).

## Power switch

The board has **no power button and no soft-power circuit**. The schematic's power section
is only an automatic USB/battery switch-over, and the regulator has no enable pin on the
headers. So there are two ways to get on/off:

1. **True off (recommended): slide switch in the battery's red wire.** This needs an
   **SS-12D00G3** (SPDT, 3-pin, 2.54 mm, 0.5 A) or the smaller **MSK-12C02**, and the
   top-edge slot is sized for it. Off means zero drain. The battery only charges over
   USB while the switch is on.
2. **Button from your Well Buying catalog: TC021-N11AAA** (side-push SMD tact,
   3.8 × 3.45 × 2.3 mm, 160 gf). It's momentary and rated 12 V / 50 mA, like everything
   in that catalog, so it **cannot** switch battery power. Wire it between a free GPIO
   and GND. Firmware then puts the board into deep sleep on a press and wakes on the
   next one. The board's regulator, USB-serial chip and LEDs stay powered, so it still
   drains slowly. This needs a firmware change and a GPIO choice.

You can fit both: slide switch for storage, button for daily sleep/wake.

## Parts

- PETG filament
- 2 × M2 × 6 self-tapping pan-head screws (or 2 × M2 heat-set inserts + 2 × M2 × 6 machine
  screws)
- LiPo 3.7 V, 502025 or smaller, with a 2-pin 1.25 mm plug. Waveshare's wiki says MX1.25
  in one place and PH1.25 in another, so match the connector on your board.
  **Check polarity against the "− +" marking on the board before plugging in.** Many
  cheap LiPos are wired reversed.
- Switch (see above)
- A scrap of foam or Kapton tape between the battery and the back of the PCB

## Check on the real parts (not verified yet)

The script was dry-run against a stub of the Fusion API, which only catches Python
errors. It has not been run in Fusion and nothing has been printed. Values marked
`ESTIMATE` in the script came from photos.

1. Window lines up with the picture: no black border showing, no pixels cut off.
   Adjust `AA_DY` / `WINDOW_INSET` if needed.
2. Board drops in without forcing and doesn't rattle (`FIT`).
3. USB-C cut-out lines up with the port and a cable seats fully (`USB_X`,
   `USB_Z_FROM_GLASS`).
4. Lid posts touch the standoffs and the glass sits flat on the brim without the lid
   bowing (`POST_PRELOAD`, `MODULE_DEPTH`).
5. Screws bite and hold. If they strip, switch to heat inserts.
6. Switch lever moves through its full travel in the slot (`SW_W`, `SW_H`).
7. Battery doesn't press on the SD card or ESP32 module.
8. Keychain: it hangs straight from the top loop, and the loop survives a firm tug
   with keys on it.
9. Belt clip: it slides onto your belt without cracking at the root and stays put
   while walking.
